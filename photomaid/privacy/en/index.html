2. Your media and files

So that you can clean up, PhotoMaid reads your photos and videos with your permission (Android permission “Photos and videos”, optionally only selected ones), along with details about them: date taken and date added, file size, video duration, dimensions, folder and whether an item is marked as a favorite. The app reads files in folders only if you explicitly grant access to a folder through the Android folder picker.

  • Everything is displayed, sorted and compared on your device. PhotoMaid does not upload media and has no server of its own.
  • To find identical copies, the app computes checksums of files. These exist only in memory during the search and are not stored.
  • Thumbnails are cached in memory only.
  • PhotoMaid uses no artificial intelligence, no image or face recognition and no analytics services.

Legal basis: providing the app feature you use (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). You can revoke the permission at any time in the Android settings.

3. What is stored on your device

  • your decisions (keep, trash, later) with a device-local identifier of each item, the history and the ability to undo
  • “Later” dates, the contents of the PhotoMaid trash and the list of connected folders
  • settings, quick access entries and collections (as filters, without media identifiers)
  • the daily removal counter, use of the Pro trial and the video bonus, the purchase status and the total storage saved so far
  • a counter that controls how often ads appear

This data is kept in the app’s protected storage. So that your decisions survive a restore, they are included in the Android backup. If you have enabled backup in your Google account, Google stores them there; you control this in the Android settings. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data removes the data from the device.

4. Deleting and moving

PhotoMaid never deletes anything automatically. A swipe to the left only puts an item in PhotoMaid’s own trash. Items are removed only when you empty that trash and Android asks you to confirm again in a system dialog. Photos and videos then go to the Android trash. Files from connected folders are deleted permanently; the app tells you beforehand. If a cloud service syncs your library, deleting can also affect your other devices. Marking favorites and moving items to a folder also go through Android system dialogs.

5. Notifications

If you allow them, PhotoMaid reminds you of items you set aside for later. Notifications are scheduled on the device and contain no file names.

6. Advertising

In the free version, PhotoMaid shows ads from Google AdMob: labeled ad cards between items, one ad in the session summary and optional video ads (for the 30-minute Pro trial or the bonus for additional removals). After purchasing Pro, no more ads are shown.

Ad requests contain no media, file names, paths, checksums or media identifiers. However, Google processes device and network data for consent checks, ad delivery, billing, fraud prevention and diagnostics. According to Google, this includes in particular:

  • the IP address, among other things for approximate location
  • device and app identifiers, possibly the Android advertising ID
  • interactions with ads, such as impressions and clicks
  • diagnostic and performance data

Whether ads are personalized depends on your consent. Google is independently responsible for this processing. Details: Google Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from apps that use its services.

Consent in the European Economic Area, Switzerland and the United Kingdom

Before ads are loaded for the first time, the app uses Google’s consent platform (UMP) to ask whether and how ads may be shown. Without that clearance, the app does not start the ads SDK and loads no ads. You can change your choice at any time in Settings under “Change ad consent”. The consent check itself already transfers technical data to Google.

Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR) where your device is accessed or ads are personalized; otherwise our legitimate interest in financing the free app (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
